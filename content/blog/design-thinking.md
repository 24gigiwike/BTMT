**The greatest of all innovation begins with a spark of an idea but generating and selecting the idea that works and makes innovation possible is where a lot of organizations get stuck.** 

Design thinking is a systematic approach to innovation that put people - their works, needs and frustrations at the center. The purpose of Design thinking is to generate ideas that truly meet the need of the people not just in assumptions or trends.

## The Systematic approach of Design Thinking is in five stages:

1. **EMPATHIZE**

Before coming up with any idea that will impact the people, there is need to identify with, understand and relate with the people. Find out what they do, what they need, the barriers, limitations and challenges they have.

2. **DEFINE**

Clearly define the target audience and what their needs truly are. The frame structure that can help you to execute the Empathize and Define stages effectively is: 

The People does (behavior) because (need) but (barrier)

In simple terms, identify and clearly define what they do, why they need to do it or what they intend to achieve with what they do and the challenges or limitations confronting them.

Eg: Elderly people wants fresh natural juice readily accessible without extra labour using electric juice extractors but they find it difficult operating the existing juice extractors.

3. **IDEATE**

This is the stage where the idea is now been crafted to meet the needs of the people. 

Having understood and celery defining what the stakeholders (people) truly need, you gather your team members and begin to creatively and critically analyze the needs and develop practicable solutions to those needs.

When these steps are actively and properly engaged, the best idea will be selected.

4. **PROTOTYPE**

After the best idea has been selected, the next thing is to create a prototype. A prototype is a quick low-cost representation of your idea to help bring it to life so audience can see, connect with and feel what the solution will be like.

The prototype to be created depends on the nature of the solution. Examples of Prototype that can be created are:

-  Cardboard or Lego (useful for physical products)
- Storyboarding - This maps the stakeholders (users) journey around the needs and the solution provided.
-  Click through wireframes (useful for digital solutions) It gives a hand-on experience.
- Concept Sketches: this shows different touch points users interact with your solution.

5. **TESTING**

After the profit has been created, it is then submitted for testing. The test can be done by deploying the following steps

 Identify the goal of the test: What are you trying to achieve with your test. This will help to discover one or all of these - desirability, feasibility and usability of the idea.

Develop a Testing Protocol: Decide the methodology through which the test will l be conducted. It could either be 

a) Closed Test: Users perform a task with a binary outcome such as either a solve or fail.

b) Open Test: Users can explore the prototype while you observe what they do.

You can either use real world users or someone who wasn’t involved in the earlier stages of the process.

**Run the test:** Let the people know it’s a test and do not over explain to avoid creating unnecessary biases. Just tell them enough to enable them complete the tasks.

 **Observe without bias:** Don’t interfere with the process so as not to give the testers an impression that you have an expected outcome. Allow testers to experience challenges while testing. 

**Follow Up:** Let testers complete a questionnaire and also give them opportunity to provide feedback beyond what you’ve asked.

 **Learn and iterate:** Observe and absorb all you can from the test and return to the earlier stages as needed.
