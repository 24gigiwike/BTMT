**Innovation is the fruit of a well-designed idea created to solve specific needs for a defined category of people.**

As a business committed to solving problems and adding value to society, you may have the resources for execution—yet generating the right idea that actually gets the job done can feel overwhelming. When faced with multiple options, teams often get stuck between competing directions. To fulfill its purpose, **ideation** cannot be left to chance; it must be strategically designed and deployed.

Below is a practical, 5-step framework for generating innovative business ideas and objectively selecting the best ones for execution.

## Step 1: Turn Customer Insights into Opportunity Statements

The empathize and define stages of design thinking provide valuable customer insights, but those insights must be reframed into actionable **opportunity statements** (often called *"How Might We"* questions) such as *"How can I…?"* or *"In what ways can we…?"*

- **The Insight:** Elderly people want fresh, natural juice readily accessible without extra labor, but they find existing electric juice extractors difficult to operate.
- **Opportunity Statement A:** How can we develop a juice extractor that is safer and more convenient for seniors to use?
- **Opportunity Statement B:** In what ways can we help elderly users operate existing juice extractors without physical difficulty?

When accurately framed, opportunity statements create clear guardrails that direct your team’s creative thinking toward real market needs.

## Step 2: Assemble a Cross-Functional Ideation Team and Set Rules

For an idea to be truly effective, it should be generated and refined by more than one perspective. Carefully select your ideation team, ideally bringing together people from different departments—such as operations, marketing, customer support, and product design—so they contribute diverse viewpoints.

Before starting, establish three ground rules:

- **Defer judgment:** There are no bad ideas during generation. Everyone should feel safe proposing unconventional solutions.
- **Aim for volume:** Set a concrete numerical target for multiple ideas before filtering anything out.
- **Enforce a time limit:** Constraints force focus and prevent overthinking.

## Step 3: Deploy a Structured Ideation Technique

Once your team and rules are in place, use one of these five proven ideation techniques to generate solutions:

### 1. The SCAMPER Method

SCAMPER is an acronym that prompts your team to look at an existing product, service, or problem from seven distinct angles:

- **Substitute:** What element, material, or process can be substituted?
- **Combine:** What ideas, features, or resources can be merged?
- **Adapt:** How could an existing solution from another industry be adapted for this problem?
- **Modify:** What can be changed, enlarged, minimized, or simplified?
- **Put to another use:** Could this idea or asset work in a completely different market or context?
- **Eliminate:** What steps or features can be removed to make the solution faster and more efficient?
- **Reverse:** What would happen if the process or sequence were reversed or rearranged?

### 2. Round Robin (The 3-Box Method)

Take a sheet of paper featuring your opportunity statement and draw three boxes labeled **Idea**, **Opportunity**, and **Evolution**.

1. Person A writes down an initial idea within 5 minutes and passes the sheet.
2. Person B identifies the idea's potential challenges and positive attributes, then passes it on.
3. Person C addresses those challenges while preserving the positives.

At the end of the cycle, each participant presents the evolved concept to the group for discussion.

### 3. Traditional Brainstorming

Team members rapidly voice out their ideas—whether complete or half-formed—while a facilitator records every suggestion without critique. Once the timer ends, the group reviews and discusses the full list. The primary goal here is generating maximum volume in a short window.

### 4. The Idea Matrix

An Idea Matrix uses a grid of columns and rows to force intersections between two sets of key factors:

- **Columns:** Focus on stakeholders, customer segments, needs, or pain points.
- **Rows:** Focus on solution enablers, such as emerging technologies, distribution channels, or market trends.

Where a column and row intersect, your team brainstorms targeted solutions tailored to that exact combination.

### 5. Brainwriting (The 6-3-5 Method)

In this silent ideation exercise, **6 people** each write down **3 ideas** within **5 minutes** on their own worksheet. When time is up, everyone passes their sheet to the next person, who builds upon those initial concepts or adds new ones. After several rounds, all worksheets are collated and reviewed together.

## Step 4: Cluster and Organize Your Ideas

Once all generated ideas are collected, group similar concepts together. Organizing raw output into themes eliminates duplicates and makes complex decision-making manageable. You can do this physically using sticky notes (**Affinity Mapping**) or digitally using collaborative whiteboards like **Miro** or **Lucidchart**.

## Step 5: Select the Best Idea for Execution

In the final decision-making stage, you need an objective filter to narrow down your clustered options without bias. Two reliable methods are:

- **The ICE Scoring Model:** Rate each idea from 1 to 10 across three criteria and calculate the average score:
  - **Impact:** How significant will the positive effect be on your target users or business goals?
  - **Confidence:** How certain are you (based on data or experience) that the solution will deliver that impact?
  - **Ease:** How fast and affordable will it be for your team to implement?
- **Dot Voting:** Give each team member a set number of votes (dots) to place on the ideas they believe are strongest. Once everyone has voted, count the dots to identify the highest-priority concepts.

With these five practical steps, your team will never get stuck staring at a blank whiteboard—moving seamlessly from customer insight to confident execution.

### Frequently Asked Questions About Ideation

- **What is the best ideation technique for quiet or remote teams?**

  Brainwriting (the 6-3-5 method) and digital Affinity Mapping on tools like Miro work best for remote or introverted teams because everyone contributes simultaneously without dominant voices taking over the room.
- **How do you narrow down too many ideas?**

  First, group overlapping concepts using Affinity Mapping. Then, score the remaining themes using the **ICE Method** (Impact, Confidence, Ease) to prioritize ideas that deliver high value with realistic execution effort.
